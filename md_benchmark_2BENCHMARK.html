Benchmark Suite for <tt>dist-m4ri</tt>

This directory contains quantum LDPC CSS parity-check matrices (.mtx) and Stim circuit-level fault-tolerance files (.stim) spanning code distances $d = 4$ to $d \le 34$. Detector Error Models (.dem) can be generated on the fly from the .stim circuits with dist_m4ri.py (noise is added to the noiseless templates with add_noise); since version 0.14.0, the binary reduces the full DEM of a circuit exactly (Section 3), so that its distance, the circuit-level distance, is found as fast as that of the DEM with the minority-basis detectors stripped.

‍[!NOTE] The distances in the file names (and in the source patterns below) are the nominal values of the sources, and they are not always correct; the file names are kept unchanged. The distances computed with dist_m4ri are listed in the tables below: an exact value is certified (by CC), and $[d_{\min}, d_{\max}]$ is a bracket from a run which hit the timeout. The values of the Mitten codes and circuits and of the full DEMs of the $X$-basis BB circuits are from the overnight benchmark of dist_m4ri 0.14.0 (128 threads, Section 5), and the brackets of the double-gross and bb_360 circuits from the follow-up runs of 0.14.1 (Section 6); the values marked "(s)" are from shorter runs of earlier versions on the stripped DEMs, and they are provisional.

Directory Structure

  • css/: MatrixMarket (.mtx) CSS parity-check matrices (_Hx.mtx, _Hz.mtx) and canonical logical operators (_Lx.mtx, _Lz.mtx for Mitten codes).
  • stim_bb/: Unmodified Bivariate Bicycle (BB), Gross, and Double-Gross .stim circuits from external repositories.
  • stim_mitten/: Mitten code .stim circuits generated via quits with CircuitBuildOptions(get_all_detectors=True).
  • stim_torus/: 2D local torus Bivariate Bicycle .stim circuits (d = 14 to 17, noiseless templates).
  • figures/: scaling of dist_m4ri with the number of threads on some of these codes and circuits (Section 4), and the time to a minimum-weight codeword in the full DEMs of the circuits (Section 5).

Origins and References

  1. **Bivariate Bicycle (BB), Gross ([[144, 12, 12]]), and Double-Gross ([[288, 12, 18]]) Codes**
    • Code Construction: S. Bravyi, A. W. Cross, J. M. Gambetta, D. Maslov, P. Rall, and T. J. Yoder, High-threshold and low-overhead fault-tolerant quantum memory, Nature 627, 778–782 (2024), arXiv:2308.07915.
    • **Higher-Distance BB Codes ([[360, 12, 24]], [[756, 16, <=34]]) & si1000 Stim Circuits**: Copied unmodified from quantumlib/tesseract-decoder (commit e7c762eef24161e304ba6fccb856a05b41f88c39, directory testdata/bivariatebicyclecodes/), associated with A. Beni et al., arXiv:2503.10988. These circuits use the superconducting-inspired si1000 noise model at $p = 0.0001$ with $r = d$ rounds in both $X$ and $Z$ memory bases.
    • **Uniform-Noise (uniform_circuit) BB Stim Circuits**: Copied unmodified from trmue/relay (commit d185194ba0cb4101ced4340d82b2ee6d42f225f0, directory tests/testdata/bicycle_bivariate/), associated with T. Müller et al., arXiv:2503.11514. These circuits use standard uniform circuit-level depolarizing noise at $p = 0.001$ with $r = d$ rounds in the $X$ memory basis.
  2. Mitten Codes (Non-Abelian Lifted-Product CSS Codes)
    • Code Construction & Stim Circuits: Generated via mkangquantum/quits (commit 0f36b8c5a9b81d6b7be0cef2c81c61b5850c0452), associated with Quantum LDPC codes for modular architectures via lifted products over non-abelian groups, arXiv:2607.28795.
    • Constructed as $1 \times 2$ lifted products $LP(A, B)$ over non-abelian groups $G = C_m \times S_3$ of order $|G| = 6m$, yielding parameters $[[5|G|, |G|, d]]$ with row weight 9 and column weight 3 or 6.
    • Circuits are generated at $p = 0.001$ (ErrorModel(1e-3, 1e-3, 1e-3, 1e-3), basis="Z", CircuitBuildOptions(get_all_detectors=True)) for two syndrome-extraction schedules:
      • custom: depth-12 interleaved mixed $X/Z$ schedule.
      • hef (hook_error_free): depth-24 group-element-layer schedule where each entangling layer is a permutation matching between one check block and one data block.
  3. 2D Local Torus Bivariate Bicycle Stim Circuits
    • Copied unmodified from tmp/*.stim. These are noiseless syndrome-extraction templates on a 2D torus with Swap/CNOT routing (L3_torus, with CZSWAP gates) for codes with the nominal parameters $[[60, 8, 14]]$, $[[120, 8, 15]]$, $[[200, 16, 16]]$, and $[[200, 8, 17]]$ (from the file names; the code distances have not been verified) in both $X$ and $Z$ bases. When passed to dist_m4ri.compute_dem_distance(circuit=...) or dist_m4ri.add_noise(circuit, p=0.001), uniform circuit-level noise is added on the fly (DEPOLARIZE2 after the two-qubit gates, including CZSWAP since 0.14.0, DEPOLARIZE1 after the one-qubit gates and on idle qubits, and X_ERROR/Z_ERROR at resets and measurements; see the README).

2.4 Circuit-Level Distances

The circuit-level distance is the minimum number of faults (error mechanisms of the DEM) which cause an undetectable logical error, i.e., the distance of the full DEM (all detectors).

  • Values without a mark: the full DEM, computed by dist_m4ri 0.14.0 in the overnight benchmark (Section 5; method=3, 128 threads), which drops the minority-basis detectors exactly (Section 3). An exact value is certified by CC: in under 6 s, except 179 s for gross and 182 s for mitten_270 custom.
  • The brackets of double_gross and bb_360 are from Section 6: the lower bounds are certified with a code-sized model of the circuit (Section 6.3), and the upper bounds are codewords of the full DEM found by RW (Sections 6.1, 6.2).
  • (s): the stripped DEM (minority-basis detectors removed), computed by earlier versions in 30 s method=3 runs on 16 threads. The stripped DEM keeps a subset of the detectors, so its distance is a lower bound on the circuit distance (for a bracket, only its lower end applies to the circuit).
  • For the noiseless torus templates, the noise of add_noise(p=0.001) is added; the circuit distance depends on the noise model (which faults are included), but not on $p$. Since 0.14.0, add_noise also adds DEPOLARIZE2 after CZSWAP, which these circuits use for routing: the values marked "old noise" are for the noise of the earlier versions (no noise after CZSWAP), and the distance with the current noise can be lower (it is the same, 6, for n60 in the $X$ basis).
  • "–": not computed yet; "n/a": no circuit for this basis.

For example, the gross-code circuit has distance 10, consistent with the circuit-level distance $\le 10$ reported for the IBM syndrome-extraction circuit, and the BB circuits with $d = 10$ have distance 8. 3. Full DEMs and Their Exact Reduction

In circuit-level noise models using DEPOLARIZE2(p), cross-term two-qubit Pauli errors (such as $X \otimes Z$, $Y \otimes X$, $Y \otimes Z$, $Y \otimes Y$) occur with probability $p/15 = O(p)$, the same order as single-type errors ($X \otimes X$ or $Z \otimes Z$). Consequently, the full DEM couples $X$-detectors and $Z$-detectors into a single large parity-check matrix with 6–8 times more distinct error columns than a single-basis projection. Since version 0.14.0, dist_m4ri merges the equal columns of a DEM and drops detectors when this is verified to be exact (drop=auto, the default; see "Dropping DEM Detectors" in the README): for every circuit below, it finds exactly the minority-basis detectors, and the matrix searched is the stripped DEM with its equal columns merged (the search for the detectors to drop takes 0.02–0.4 s, and 1.2 s for bb_756). Hence the distance of the full DEM, the circuit-level distance, is found as fast as that of the stripped DEM, which is only a lower bound on it.

To avoid storing large pre-generated .dem files in the repository, DEMs can be generated on the fly from the .stim files (noise of strength p_noise=0.001 is added to the noiseless templates; --force-noise replaces the noise of a circuit by this uniform noise):

import dist_m4ri
# the full DEM of the circuit, reduced exactly by the binary; also save the reduced DEM and its maps to the full one

circuit="benchmark/stim_torus/NNEESEEESEENN_L3_torus_s1_NW_w10p_s2_SW_w6p_n60_k8_d14_X.stim",
out_dem="tmp/torus_n60_X_full.dem", out_reduced="tmp/torus_n60_X") # tmp/torus_n60_X_red.dem, _red.json

6.3 Certified lower bounds from a code-sized model

For a memory circuit, a certified lower bound needs no CC on the DEM:

  1. The checks of the measured basis are read from the detectors after the final data measurement: the final data measurements in such a detector give the support of a check.
  2. One observable per check is added to the circuit: the parity of the final data measurements on its support. It is deterministic, and it is flipped by an error mechanism iff the net effect of the error on the data anticommutes with the check.
  3. The rows $O$ of these observables in the DEM are verified to lie in the row space of the detector matrix $H$: $\mathrm{rank}([H; O]) = \mathrm{rank}(H)$. Then every codeword $x$ of the DEM ($Hx = 0$, $Lx \neq 0$) gives a codeword of the model $(O, L)$ (equal columns merged) of weight at most $|x|$.
  4. The distance of the model, computed by CC with the binary, is therefore a lower bound on the circuit distance.

The model has one column per net effect of a single error on the checks and the observables: about $2.5 n$ columns (the data qubits and the hook errors), with 3–6 nonzeros each. The verification held for all circuits below ($X$ basis).
