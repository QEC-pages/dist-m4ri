Benchmark Suite for dist-m4ri

This directory contains quantum LDPC CSS parity-check matrices (.mtx) and Stim circuit-level fault-tolerance files (.stim) spanning code distances $d = 4$ to $d \le 34$. Detector Error Models (.dem) can be generated on the fly from the .stim circuits with dist_m4ri.py (noise is added to the noiseless templates with add_noise); since version 0.14.0, the binary reduces the full DEM of a circuit exactly (Section 3), so that its distance, the circuit-level distance, is found as fast as that of the DEM with the minority-basis detectors stripped.

Note
The distances in the file names (and in the source patterns below) are the nominal values of the sources, and they are not always correct; the file names are kept unchanged. The distances computed with dist_m4ri are listed in the tables below: an exact value is certified (by CC), and $[d_{\min}, d_{\max}]$ is a bracket from a run which hit the timeout. The values of the Mitten codes and circuits and of the full DEMs of the $X$-basis BB circuits are from the overnight benchmark of dist_m4ri 0.14.0 (128 threads, Section 5), and the brackets of the double-gross and bb_360 circuits from the follow-up runs of 0.14.1 (Section 6); the values marked "(s)" are from shorter runs of earlier versions on the stripped DEMs, and they are provisional. The values of the codes and circuits added on 2026-10-20 (the yarn_* Mitten codes and circuits, the SHYPS circuits, the $Z$-basis uniform-noise BB circuits) and the re-runs of the uniform-noise gross circuit and of the torus circuits (Section 2.6) are from short runs of 0.14.7. The distances of the larger codes of Section 1 (the yarn_* codes with $d \ge 16$, bb_360, and bb_756) are from the branch-and-bound CC search of 0.15.0 (Section 9; those up to $d = 18$ confirmed with the walk of the earlier versions, Section 10.1). The 2bga_* codes and circuits added on 2026-11-05 (Origins, item 5) were only checked in short RW runs of 0.15.2: the distances in their prefixes are the exact values of their source database.

Directory Structure

  • css/: MatrixMarket (.mtx) CSS parity-check matrices (_Hx.mtx, _Hz.mtx) and canonical logical operators (_Lx.mtx, _Lz.mtx for the Mitten codes, including the yarn_* codes of arXiv:2607.28795, and for the 2bga_* codes, which also have logical operators of the minimum weight in .nz files, _wX.nz and _wZ.nz).
  • stim_bb/: Unmodified Bivariate Bicycle (BB), Gross, and Double-Gross .stim circuits from external repositories.
  • stim_mitten/: Mitten code .stim circuits generated via quits with CircuitBuildOptions(get_all_detectors=True) (mitten_*), and from the published hook-free schedules of arXiv:2607.28795 with make_yarn_mitten_circuits.py (yarn_*).
  • stim_shyps/: Unmodified SHYPS code .stim circuits ($Z$-basis memory).
  • stim_2bga/: Unmodified .stim circuits of two two-block group-algebra (2BGA) codes (memory in both bases).
  • stim_torus/: 2D local torus Bivariate Bicycle .stim circuits (d = 14 to 17, noiseless templates).
  • figures/: scaling of dist_m4ri with the number of threads on some of these codes and circuits (Section 4), the time to a minimum-weight codeword in the full DEMs of the circuits (Sections 5 and 8), and the lightest codeword found against time with and without the RW pairs (Section 8).

Origins and References

  1. Bivariate Bicycle (BB), Gross ([[144, 12, 12]]), and Double-Gross ([[288, 12, 18]]) Codes
    • Code Construction: S. Bravyi, A. W. Cross, J. M. Gambetta, D. Maslov, P. Rall, and T. J. Yoder, High-threshold and low-overhead fault-tolerant quantum memory, Nature 627, 778–782 (2024), arXiv:2308.07915.
    • Higher-Distance BB Codes ([[360, 12, 24]], [[756, 16, <=34]]) & si1000 Stim Circuits: Copied unmodified from quantumlib/tesseract-decoder (commit e7c762eef24161e304ba6fccb856a05b41f88c39, directory testdata/bivariatebicyclecodes/), associated with A. Beni et al., arXiv:2503.10988. These circuits use the superconducting-inspired si1000 noise model at $p = 0.0001$ with $r = d$ rounds in both $X$ and $Z$ memory bases.
    • Uniform-Noise (uniform_circuit) BB Stim Circuits: Copied unmodified from trmue/relay (commit d185194ba0cb4101ced4340d82b2ee6d42f225f0, directory tests/testdata/bicycle_bivariate/), associated with T. Müller et al., arXiv:2503.11514. These circuits use standard uniform circuit-level depolarizing noise at $p = 0.001$ with $r = d$ rounds in the $X$ and $Z$ memory bases (memory_X, memory_Z; the $Z$-basis circuits were added on 2026-10-20, from the same commit).
  2. Mitten Codes (Non-Abelian Lifted-Product CSS Codes)
    • Code Construction & Stim Circuits: Generated via mkangquantum/quits (commit 0f36b8c5a9b81d6b7be0cef2c81c61b5850c0452); the Mitten codes were introduced in High-rate qLDPC processors, arXiv:2607.28795.
    • Constructed as $1 \times 2$ lifted products $LP(A, B)$ over non-abelian groups $G = C_m \times S_3$ of order $|G| = 6m$, yielding parameters $[[5|G|, |G|, d]]$ with row weight 9 and column weight 3 or 6.
    • Circuits are generated at $p = 0.001$ (ErrorModel(1e-3, 1e-3, 1e-3, 1e-3), basis="Z", CircuitBuildOptions(get_all_detectors=True)) for two syndrome-extraction schedules:
      • custom: depth-12 interleaved mixed $X/Z$ schedule.
      • hef (hook_error_free): depth-24 group-element-layer schedule where each entangling layer is a permutation matching between one check block and one data block.
    • Codes of arXiv:2607.28795 and their hook-free circuits (yarn_*): the check matrices and paired logical bases of processor_codes/ (8 mitten, 6 structured_mitten, and 1 abelian_poly_LP code) of a7b/yarn, the repository of the paper (commit 1b58edd24e19b6b5da6814fd3a74ebb376b6e90f, MIT license), converted from .npy to .mtx (css/yarn_*). The circuits stim_mitten/yarn_*_hookfree_r<d>_{X,Z}.stim are generated by stim_mitten/make_yarn_mitten_circuits.py from the published hook-free syndrome-extraction schedules (hook_free_SE_cycle_schedule.json, for 13 of the codes): each cycle runs the 12 CX layers of the $X$-check round, measures the $X$ checks, and then runs the 12 layers of the $Z$-check round; $r = d$ rounds, memory in the $X$ and $Z$ bases, and the noise of dist_m4ri.add_noise(p=0.001). The DEM of the $X$-basis circuit of $[[150, 30, 10]]$ has exactly the 76,245 distinct error mechanisms of the paper's DEM telescoping-decoder/tests/data/paper_nonab_150_30_10_xyz_p0.001_r10_xinit_L1_hookfree.dem.
  3. 2D Local Torus Bivariate Bicycle Stim Circuits
    • Copied unmodified from tmp/*.stim. These are noiseless syndrome-extraction templates on a 2D torus with Swap/CNOT routing (L3_torus, with CZSWAP gates) for codes with the nominal parameters $[[60, 8, 14]]$, $[[120, 8, 15]]$, $[[200, 16, 16]]$, and $[[200, 8, 17]]$ (from the file names; the code distances have not been verified) in both $X$ and $Z$ bases. When passed to dist_m4ri.compute_dem_distance(circuit=...) or dist_m4ri.add_noise(circuit, p=0.001), uniform circuit-level noise is added on the fly (DEPOLARIZE2 after the two-qubit gates, including CZSWAP since 0.14.0, DEPOLARIZE1 after the one-qubit gates and on idle qubits, and X_ERROR/Z_ERROR at resets and measurements; see the README).
  4. SHYPS Codes (Subsystem Hypergraph Product Simplex Codes)
    • Memory circuits copied unmodified from PhotonicInc/ComputingEfficientlyInQLDPCCodes (commit df815862d22dd02d3c3a7f7266639ac5b7840a91, MIT license, directories src/stim_circuits/shyps_r4_memory_circuits/ and src/stim_circuits/shyps_r5_memory_circuits/, the files ..._one_codeblock_memory_simulation_Z_detectors_circuit_p_0.001.stim), associated with A. J. Malcolm et al., Computing Efficiently in QLDPC Codes, arXiv:2502.07150.
    • One code block of the $[[225, 16, 8]]$ ($r = 4$) and $[[961, 25, 16]]$ ($r = 5$) SHYPS codes: $Z$-basis memory with detectors of the $Z$ type only, $d + 1$ rounds of gauge measurements, and circuit-level noise at $p = 0.001$ (DEPOLARIZE2 after the CX gates, flips at the resets and measurements, no idle noise). The repository has no $X$-basis memory circuits of these codes.
  5. Two-Block Group-Algebra (2BGA) Codes of the Bicycle Race Database
    • Codes and Stim circuits: from gideonlsx/bicycle-race (commit cee978f8b92256a4783b7e0792f0207a542a1b1e), the companion database of G. Lee, Bicycle Race: small two-block codes on the Pareto frontier, arXiv:2610.13880 (2026); its data are licensed under CC BY 4.0.
    • A code $C(a, b)$ over a finite group $G$ (elements $e_0, e_1, \ldots$ numbered as in GAP's Elements(SmallGroup(o, i)), $e_0$ the identity) has $n = 2|G|$ qubits, $H_X = (L(a) \mid R(b))$, and $H_Z = (R(b)^T \mid L(a)^T)$, where $L(a)$ ($R(b)$) has a 1 in row $s g$ ($g s$) and column $g$ for each $s$ in $a$ ($b$). The check matrices were rebuilt with the database's scripts/codes.py (which checks the commutation, $k$, and the database's logical operators of weight $d_X$ and $d_Z$); the comment lines of the files give $a$, $b$, and the database id of each code. The paired logical bases _Lx.mtx, _Lz.mtx ($L_X L_Z^T = I$) were computed here, and _wX.nz, _wZ.nz are the database's logical operators of weight $d_X$ and $d_Z$ (.nz list format, 1-based columns, for finC).
    • Seven codes with check weights 5 to 9 and distances 10 to 23, four of them over non-abelian groups. Their distances are the exact values of the database, from the paper's connected-cluster engine. The database also reports dist-m4ri certificates: of both sectors for 2bga_102_20_10_C51, and for 2bga_120_24_10_C30xC2 and 2bga_288_4_20_C24xS3 by CC from the symmetry roots of a checked automorphism group with two orbits.
    • Circuits (Fig. 3b of the paper, copied unmodified and renamed): $X$- and $Z$-basis memory of 2bga_102_20_10_C51 and 2bga_120_24_10_C30xC2, 10 rounds of a "bare, equivariant, sequential" schedule with one ancilla per check, and the idle-free noise model of Zhao et al. (arXiv:2604.16209, App. B.1) at $p = 0.001$ (DEPOLARIZE1 after the preparations and before the measurements, DEPOLARIZE2 after the CX gates, no idle noise). The header of each file describes the circuit and gives the circuit-level distance 8.

  • The brackets of double_gross and bb_360 are from Section 6: the lower bounds are certified with a code-sized model of the circuit (Section 6.3), and the upper bounds are codewords of the full DEM found by RW (Sections 6.1, 6.2).

3. Full DEMs and Their Exact Reduction

In circuit-level noise models using DEPOLARIZE2(p), cross-term two-qubit Pauli errors (such as $X \otimes Z$, $Y \otimes X$, $Y \otimes Z$, $Y \otimes Y$) occur with probability $p/15 = O(p)$, the same order as single-type errors ($X \otimes X$ or $Z \otimes Z$). Consequently, the full DEM couples $X$-detectors and $Z$-detectors into a single large parity-check matrix with 6–8 times more distinct error columns than a single-basis projection. Since version 0.14.0, dist_m4ri merges the equal columns of a DEM and drops detectors when this is verified to be exact (drop=auto, the default; see "Dropping DEM Detectors" in the README): for every circuit below with detectors of both bases (all but the SHYPS circuits, whose detectors are all of the $Z$ type), it finds exactly the minority-basis detectors, and the matrix searched is the stripped DEM with its equal columns merged (the search for the detectors to drop takes 0.02–0.4 s, and 1.2 s for bb_756, with 0.14.0). Hence the distance of the full DEM, the circuit-level distance, is found as fast as that of the stripped DEM, which is only a lower bound on it.

To avoid storing large pre-generated .dem files in the repository, DEMs can be generated on the fly from the .stim files (noise of strength p_noise=0.001 is added to the noiseless templates; --force-noise replaces the noise of a circuit by this uniform noise):

6.3 Certified lower bounds from a code-sized model

For a memory circuit, a certified lower bound needs no CC on the DEM:

  1. The checks of the measured basis are read from the detectors after the final data measurement: the final data measurements in such a detector give the support of a check.
  2. One observable per check is added to the circuit: the parity of the final data measurements on its support. It is deterministic, and it is flipped by an error mechanism iff the net effect of the error on the data anticommutes with the check.
  3. The rows $O$ of these observables in the DEM are verified to lie in the row space of the detector matrix $H$: $\mathrm{rank}([H; O]) = \mathrm{rank}(H)$. Then every codeword $x$ of the DEM ($Hx = 0$, $Lx \neq 0$) gives a codeword of the model $(O, L)$ (equal columns merged) of weight at most $|x|$.
  4. The distance of the model, computed by CC with the binary, is therefore a lower bound on the circuit distance.

The model has one column per net effect of a single error on the checks and the observables: about $2.5 n$ columns (the data qubits and the hook errors), with 3–6 nonzeros each. The verification held for all circuits below ($X$ basis).

7.2 Window size of the hybrid RW steps

A step finds a codeword if all but one of its columns are pivots. 8.7 A work bound for the RW pairs (0.14.6)

0.14.6 fixes the slow early steps of the RW pairs found above (mitten_270 in Section 8.1, bb_756 in Section 8.2):

  • With pairs=-1 (auto), $l$ is at least the $l$ at which the about $(n-r)^2/2^{l+1}$ pairs of a step are within about the work of its elimination, $rn/16$ pairs (or $2^{20}$ pairs for small matrices). Early in a run, while the upper bound is far above the distance, the keep probability alone gives $l = 1$ (all pairs in two buckets); the bound is $l \ge 6$ for the reduced DEMs of mitten_270 ($r = 648$, $n = 8910$) and bb_756 ($r = 13222$, $n = 129276$), 5 for gross, 4 for mitten_150 and surf_d7, and 1 (no change) for the codes of Section 8.3 and the small matrices of Section 8.4.
  • The stop flags and the upper bound are also polled within the buckets of the pairs (every 65536 pairs), so that a run ends at its timeout also with few rows (e.g., pairs=1).
